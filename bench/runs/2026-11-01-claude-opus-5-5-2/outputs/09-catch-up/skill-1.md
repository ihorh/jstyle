You're taking over tomasz's open follow-up on issue #42 (`issues/42-quoting.md`). Here's what you need from the thread.

**The original bug.** Before this fix, `read_row()` ended a row at the first newline and didn't look at quotes. maya reported that this breaks CSVs whose quoted fields contain line breaks, such as CRM exports with a `notes` column. One record came out as two broken rows. Two teams, billing and support analytics, are waiting on the fix. devi had left quotes out on purpose in 0.1. A reader that tracks quotes can mistake one stray `"` for the start of a field, and then that field runs to the end of the file. devi's first try at quote tracking had no limit on row size, and that's exactly what happened. A 400k-row test file with one stray quote in row 12 came out as a header and one enormous row.

**What got merged.** `read_row()` now tracks quotes, so a newline inside quotes stays in its field. Each row is capped by `--max-row-bytes`, which defaults to 65536. When a row passes the cap, `read_row()` keeps the part read so far and drops the rest of the line. It returns `(row, True)`, and the `True` marks the row as truncated. `split_file()` still writes that shortened row to the chunk and counts it. At the end of the run, the CLI prints this to stderr: `splitlane: warning: N row(s) longer than M bytes were truncated`. maya's teams check for that warning in their scripts, so whatever you change has to keep it. The upshot is that a stray quote now damages one row instead of the rest of the file. The fix is listed under Unreleased in `CHANGELOG.md`. 0.4.0 hasn't shipped yet. It's held on #57, a Windows-only bug that has nothing to do with this one.

**Your follow-up.** After a truncation, the reader skips ahead to the next newline (`src/splitlane/split.py:24-29`), and it doesn't check whether that newline is inside quotes. If the newline sits inside a real multi-line field, the reader starts the next row in the middle of that field. tomasz called this probably rare and not blocking for 0.4.0, but nobody has measured how rare. The thread doesn't set a deadline or a scope either, so it's up to you whether to fix it before 0.4.0 or after.

What follows is my own reading of the code, and nobody on the thread has confirmed it. Once the reader starts mid-field, it treats the rest of that field as a new row. It then takes the field's closing quote for an opening one and reads everything after it as quoted text. If it reaches the byte cap first, that's a second truncation. If it finds another `"` first, the rows in between get merged into one. To check, set a small `--max-row-bytes` so a stray quote hits the cap quickly, and put a multi-line quoted field right where the skip lands. I didn't find any tests in the repo, so yours would be the first one.
